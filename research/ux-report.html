Frontend UX Research & Design Report

Designing the ideal mobility app for Casablanca

A competitive teardown of the world's leading Mobility-as-a-Service, ride-hailing and transit apps — distilled into a clean, accessible, mobile-first design system for CasaMotion.

15 apps analysed 20 UX dimensions 30+ screens designed Mobile-first PWA Grounded in the live FastAPI

Executive summary

CasaMotion already has a production streaming backend (Kafka → Flink → Cassandra) and a 7-route FastAPI, but no rider-facing frontend. This report benchmarks the best mobility apps in the world and proposes a design that turns those live capabilities — taxi matching, demand forecasting, live vehicle positions across 16 Casablanca zones — into a delightful, low-friction experience, with a clear path to full multimodal transit.

What the best apps share

  • Map-first home with a bottom sheet that owns all actions
  • One dominant "Where to?" search entry point
  • 3–5 tab bottom navigation, labelled, always visible
  • Progressive disclosure — commit to price/route only when ready
  • Live status as first-class UI (ETA, driver, arrivals)

Where most apps still fail

  • Cluttered home screens with competing promos
  • Weak empty / loading / error states
  • Accessibility as an afterthought (contrast, targets, RTL)
  • Payment steps that surprise users with hidden fees
  • Multimodal options buried below ride-hailing upsells

CasaMotion's opportunity

  • A truly unified taxi + tram + bus + train + bike planner
  • Trust-first pricing (MAD tariffs, no surge surprises)
  • Arabic / French / English + full RTL from day one
  • Carbon & equity insights native to the journey
  • Works as an installable PWA on low-end Android
Top recommendation: ship a map-first PWA whose first release wires only the four real endpoints — /zones, /vehicles/{zone_id}, /demand/forecast, /trips — behind a design system that is already multimodal-ready, so tram/bus/train/bike/payments slot in without redesign.

Methodology & scoring

Each app was assessed against 20 UX dimensions. Scores are expert heuristic ratings (1–5) informed by first-hand app knowledge and verified against official design guidance (Apple HIG, Google Material 3) and vendor product pages. They are directional, not laboratory usability metrics.

App profiles — 15 leading mobility apps

Fifteen leading apps, shown as a logo wall — hover any logo to read its full profile (what users see first, why the UX works, what to improve, and the signature idea worth stealing). The letter grade is the holistic UX rating; the italic number is display order, not a ranking.

Ride-hailing

U

Uber

Ride-hailing · super-app
A

Sees first

Full-screen map with a single "Where to?" bar docked on a bottom sheet, plus saved shortcuts (Home/Work) and product tiles.

Why it works

The bottom sheet is the entire app; one search field funnels intent, then progressively reveals options, price and ETA only when relevant.

Improve

Home is increasingly cluttered with Eats/Rent promos; surge pricing feels opaque and can erode trust at commit time.

Signature idea

Upfront fixed fare shown before booking, plus a slick live "car approaching" map with driver + plate + ETA.

B

Bolt

Ride-hailing · scooters
A-

Sees first

Clean map + "Where to?" with category chips (ride tiers, scooters) surfaced fast; lighter than Uber.

Why it works

Fewer distractions, snappy performance on low-end phones, transparent price breakdown — strong fit for emerging markets.

Improve

Live tracking map polish and driver-comms lag behind Uber; occasional inconsistent iconography.

Signature idea

Category chips that switch ride/scooter/e-bike without leaving the home sheet — multimodal in one tap.

L

Lyft

Ride-hailing (US)
B+

Sees first

Warm, friendly map with a prominent destination search and a playful, rounded visual language.

Why it works

Strong brand personality, clear tier comparison, integrated transit + bikeshare in some cities gives a light MaaS feel.

Improve

US-only; the friendly styling sometimes sacrifices information density and quick scanning.

Signature idea

Transit + bike + scooter tabs alongside rides — an early attempt at ride-hailing-as-MaaS.

C

Careem

Ride-hailing · super-app (MENA)
B+

Sees first

Super-app tile grid (rides, food, delivery, payments) above a map entry — MENA-localised, Arabic + RTL native.

Why it works

Deep regional relevance: cash option, Arabic-first, local payment rails, captain (driver) trust cues. Most relevant peer for Morocco.

Improve

The tile-grid home dilutes the core "get a ride" task; can feel heavy and slow on older devices.

Signature idea

First-class Arabic/RTL + cash-friendly flows and "Careem Pay" wallet baked into the journey.

iD

inDrive

Ride-hailing · price-negotiation
B

Sees first

A form to set your own fare for a trip, then wait for drivers to accept or counter-offer.

Why it works

Fare transparency and rider agency resonate strongly in price-sensitive markets; no opaque surge.

Improve

Negotiation adds friction and cognitive load; matching can be slow; map/live-tracking polish is basic.

Signature idea

Rider-proposed pricing — a powerful trust mechanic for cash-first, informal markets like Casablanca's taxis.

G

Grab

Super-app (SE Asia)
A-

Sees first

A super-app dashboard: transport, food, mart, wallet — a colourful service grid with a persistent GrabPay balance.

Why it works

One account, one wallet, everything; excellent localisation, cash + wallet parity, strong safety features.

Improve

Feature overload; the ride task competes with many services; heavy app footprint.

Signature idea

Wallet-centric everything + "GrabRewards" loyalty woven through every trip.

Transit & multimodal planners

Cm

Citymapper

Multimodal transit planner
A

Sees first

"Get me somewhere" with big saved destinations and a clean, colour-coded list of every way to get there.

Why it works

Effortless multimodal comparison (walk/bus/metro/bike/rain-safe/cheapest), delightful microcopy, and step-by-step GO mode with live disruptions.

Improve

Best features gated behind CLUB subscription; city coverage is limited vs Google.

"Rain-safe" and "easy" route options, plus lock-screen live navigation — routing framed around human comfort, not just speed.

Mv

Moovit

Transit · 3,500 cities
A-

Sees first

A search-led home with nearby lines and stops; the world's widest transit coverage (1.7B users, 112 countries).

Why it works

Crowd-sourced real-time data reaches cities others ignore; strong accessibility mode and step-by-step arrivals.

Improve

Interface feels ad-heavy and dated; visual hierarchy is busier than Citymapper/Transit.

Signature idea

Community-reported live conditions + a low-carbon commute program — a proven model for data-scarce cities like Casablanca.

Ts

Transit

Transit planner (design-led)
A

Sees first

A gorgeous "departures near you" board — nearby lines colour-coded with live countdowns, before you even search.

Why it works

Best-in-class visual hierarchy and typography; answers "when's my bus?" in zero taps; superb GO trip companion.

Improve

North-America-centric; fewer super-app features (no in-app ride-hail in most cities).

Signature idea

Nearby-departures-first home screen — the single strongest "glanceable transit" pattern to emulate.

GM

Google Maps

Maps · universal planner
A

Sees first

A rich, explorable map with a search bar and mode tabs (drive/transit/walk/bike/rideshare) once a destination is set.

Why it works

Universal coverage, unmatched POI data, reliable transit + live traffic, and aggregated ride-hail prices in one view.

Improve

Information-dense to the point of overwhelming; discovery/Explore competes with core navigation.

Signature idea

Mode-comparison tabs with time + price + carbon side-by-side, and "leave now / arrive by" scheduling.

AM

Apple Maps

Maps · transit & drive
A-

Sees first

A calm, low-clutter map with a floating search sheet; refined typography and generous whitespace.

Why it works

Exemplary restraint, privacy-forward, buttery map interactions, and clear transit line rendering; a masterclass in the bottom-sheet pattern.

Improve

Transit coverage narrower than Google; fewer power features; no ride-hail aggregation.

Signature idea

The definitive multi-detent bottom sheet + "Liquid Glass" material and privacy-first data handling.

Wz

Waze

Navigation · driving
B+

Sees first

A driving-optimised map covered in community reports (police, hazards, jams) with a big search + "Go".

Why it works

Community-sourced live traffic is unbeatable for drivers; gamified reporting builds a loyal contributor base.

Improve

Visually noisy; driving-only (no transit/walk); not suited to multimodal planning.

Signature idea

Frictionless one-tap community hazard reporting — a template for crowd-sourced live traffic in Casablanca.

SNCF Connect

Rail ticketing + planner (FR)
B

Sees first

A door-to-door planner with a bold search and integrated ticket purchase for trains + regional transit.

Why it works

Combines planning, booking, ticketing and live disruption in one French-market flow; strong dark theme.

Improve

Heavy and occasionally sluggish; the 2022 redesign drew criticism for hidden functions and overload.

Signature idea

End-to-end "plan → buy ticket → board → live status" for rail — the model for integrating ONCF trains.

RB

RATP Bonjour

Urban transit + ticketing (Paris)
B

Sees first

A metro/bus/RER planner with line status, nearby stops, and in-app ticket purchase for the Paris network.

Why it works

Authoritative operator data, clear line-status board, and mobile ticketing (NFC) integrated into the journey.

Improve

Cluttered and inconsistent; multiple overlapping RATP apps confuse users; onboarding is weak.

Signature idea

Operator-grade real-time line-status board + NFC ticketing — the reference for Casa Tramway integration.

Gj

Gojek

Super-app (Indonesia)
A-

Sees first

A vibrant service grid (ride, car, food, pay, more) with GoPay balance front-and-centre.

Why it works

Motorbike-taxi (ojek) first — perfectly matched to dense, congested cities; wallet + cash parity; huge service breadth.

Improve

Dashboard overload; the ride task is one of many; can overwhelm new users.

Signature idea

Two-wheeler ride tier + deeply integrated wallet — a blueprint for informal, congestion-heavy transport.

Reading the grades: A = best-in-class experience worth directly emulating; B = solid but with notable friction. Careem, inDrive, Grab and Gojek are weighted heavily in CasaMotion's synthesis because their markets (cash-first, RTL, informal transport, congestion) most resemble Casablanca.

Cross-app pattern synthesis

Three lenses distilled from all fifteen apps: the conventions everyone converges on, the unique ideas worth borrowing, and the specific choices that measurably reduce friction.

Consistent patterns

Present in nearly every top app — deviate only with strong reason.

  • Map-first canvas with a draggable bottom sheet as the control surface
  • One "Where to?" search as the primary call-to-action
  • 3–5 labelled tabs in a persistent bottom bar (Apple HIG / Material)
  • Home & Work shortcuts above recent destinations
  • Progressive disclosure: map → options → review → confirm
  • Live status is a screen, not a toast (ETA, vehicle, arrivals)
  • 48dp touch targets, 8dp spacing, high-contrast text
  • Colour-coded transit lines matching official operator colours

Unique ideas

Signature mechanics from a single app that could differentiate CasaMotion.

  • Rain-safe / easy routes (Citymapper) — comfort-based ranking
  • Rider-set fares (inDrive) — agency & price trust
  • Departures-first home (Transit) — zero-tap answers
  • One-tap hazard reports (Waze) — crowd-sourced traffic
  • Two-wheeler tier (Gojek) — congestion-beating option
  • Lock-screen live nav (Citymapper) — glance without unlocking
  • Low-carbon commute program (Moovit) — sustainability as a feature
  • Wallet-centric everything (Grab) — balance always visible

Friction reducers

Concrete choices that shorten the path to "I'm on my way".

  • Guest / deferred login — browse & plan before signing in
  • Upfront price shown before commit — no surprises
  • Autocomplete + recents ranked by frequency & proximity
  • Sticky primary button that updates as choices are made
  • Optimistic UI + skeletons instead of blank spinners
  • Actionable errors ("Retry", "Call driver") not dead ends
  • Saved places & one-tap rebook from history
  • Offline-tolerant caching of last-known map & trips
The winning formula: a map-first, sheet-driven home that answers "when & how do I get there?" at a glance, a single search funnel with progressive disclosure, transparent pricing, live status as a first-class screen, and rock-solid empty/loading/error states — all accessible and localised.

Pattern mockups

Original, hand-built recreations of the strongest patterns (no third-party screenshots). These illustrate the conventions CasaMotion adopts, annotated with why they work.

Why it works (Uber · Apple Maps): the map is context, the sheet is control. A single "Where to?", Home/Work shortcuts, and multimodal tiles put every core action one tap away without hiding the city.
Why it works (Transit · Moovit): answers "how do I leave right now?" in zero taps. CasaMotion powers this directly from /vehicles/{zone_id} and /demand/forecast, with live countdowns and operator-coloured line badges.
Why it works (Citymapper · Google Maps): side-by-side comparison of time, price and carbon with human-friendly tags (Fastest / Greenest / Cheapest). Users choose on their priority, not just speed.
Why it works (Uber): a persistent ETA banner, a live vehicle on the route, and a driver card with plate + one-tap call/message build trust. CasaMotion draws vehicle position from Cassandra via /vehicles/{zone_id}.

States that most apps neglect

Empty, loading and error states are where good apps become great. CasaMotion treats each as a designed screen with a clear message and a next action.

State design rules: every empty state offers a next step; loading uses content-shaped skeletons + optimistic UI; errors name the problem in plain language and always give a recovery action (Retry / Continue offline / Call). This directly addresses the weakest column in the matrix for most apps.

CasaMotion — proposed frontend design

Everything above converges here. CasaMotion is a map-first, sheet-driven, mobile-first PWA that unifies taxi, tramway, bus, train, bike and walking into one trustworthy journey — clean like Apple Maps, glanceable like Transit, multimodal like Citymapper, and localised like Careem.

Design principles

  • Map is context, sheet is control
  • One question at a time — progressive disclosure
  • Trust by default — upfront MAD pricing, no surge tricks
  • Glanceable — answers before taps
  • Inclusive — AR/FR/EN, RTL, WCAG AA
  • Resilient — offline-tolerant, low-end friendly

Grounded in the live backend

Phase-1 UI wires only endpoints that exist today.

  • GET /zones → zone picker & map regions live
  • GET /vehicles/{zone_id} → live taxis & tracking live
  • POST /demand/forecast → "busy now / wait time" live
  • POST /trips → request a ride live
  • POST /auth/token → sign-in live

Designed-ahead (no backend yet)

UI is ready; these light up as services land.

  • Tram / bus / train schedules & ticketing planned
  • Bike-share docks & unlock planned
  • Digital payments & wallet planned
  • Push notifications & live disruptions planned
  • Carbon insights & saved places sync planned
Honesty guardrail: multimodal transit, payments, bike-share, notifications and carbon tracking are design-forward proposals, not currently implemented in the CasaMotion backend. Only taxi matching, demand forecasting, live vehicle positions and zones are live today.

Foundations

Spacing (4-pt grid)

Radius

sm 8 · md 14 · lg 22 · xl 30 · pill 999

Elevation

Three shadow levels: cards (subtle), sheets (medium), modals/FAB (strong).

Touch & contrast (verified)

  • Min touch target 48×48dp, 8dp apart (Material 3)
  • Text contrast ≥ 4.5:1, large text ≥ 3:1 (WCAG AA)
  • Bottom bar: ≤5 labelled tabs, always visible (Apple HIG)
  • Badges reserved for critical/live info only

Motion & interaction

Sheet detents

Bottom sheet snaps to peek / half / full (Apple Maps model). Map stays interactive behind it.

Timing

150–250ms ease-out for transitions; respect prefers-reduced-motion and offer a "reduce motion" toggle.

Feedback

Optimistic UI on booking; haptic + toast on confirm; skeletons for loads; inline validation on forms.

Component hierarchy

An atomic-design structure. Primitives compose into patterns, patterns compose into the 31 screens — keeping the codebase small and consistent.

Reuse in numbers

~8 atoms + ~6 molecules + ~5 organisms + ~5 templates compose all 31 screens. High reuse = smaller bundle (important for a low-end-Android PWA) and visual consistency for free.

Suggested stack

  • React + Vite (or Vue) PWA, TypeScript
  • MapLibre GL (open-source, no vendor lock, RTL text plugin)
  • Tailwind config seeded with the tokens above
  • Workbox service worker for offline/last-known map
  • i18next for AR/FR/EN + dir="rtl" switching
